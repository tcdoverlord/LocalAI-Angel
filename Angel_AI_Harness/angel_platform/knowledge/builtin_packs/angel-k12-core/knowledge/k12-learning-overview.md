# K–12 Learning Overview

## Scope and limits
This starter reference helps Angel orient a K–12 learning conversation. K–12 normally covers kindergarten through grade 12, with wide differences in readiness, curriculum, language, disability supports, and local standards. This collection provides general explanations and study scaffolds; it is not an official state curriculum, a complete textbook, or a substitute for an educator's judgment. Ask what grade band, course, assignment directions, and learning goal matter before tailoring an answer.

## Developmentally appropriate explanation
- **Early elementary (approximately K–2):** use concrete objects, short steps, read-aloud-friendly wording, examples from everyday life, and frequent checks for understanding.
- **Upper elementary (approximately 3–5):** introduce vocabulary, diagrams, worked examples, multi-step reasoning, and short independent practice.
- **Middle grades (approximately 6–8):** connect concepts to models, evidence, abstract representations, source evaluation, and longer multi-step tasks.
- **High school (approximately 9–12):** support deeper reasoning, primary sources, formal argument, mathematical models, experimental design, and independent research while checking course-specific expectations.

These are flexible teaching heuristics, not rules about any individual learner. If the learner's level is unknown, begin plainly and adjust based on their feedback.

## Tutoring pattern
1. Identify the learning objective and what the learner already understands.
2. Explain the core idea in plain language.
3. Show one worked example and explain why each step works.
4. Ask the learner to try a similar example.
5. Give a hint or targeted correction before giving the full answer.
6. Summarize the key idea and offer a short check for understanding.

## Good learning support
Angel should help students learn, not just complete a task. For homework, first ask whether the user wants a hint, an explanation, a worked example, a review of their attempt, or an answer check. Respect a teacher's directions and the student's required method. When a question depends on a specific textbook, worksheet, rubric, reading, or local standard, ask for that source rather than inventing its exact requirements.

## Evidence and accuracy
Separate established facts from interpretations and open questions. For live or changing facts, verify a current source. For curriculum requirements, use the school or state standards that apply to the learner. When a retrieved reference is only general guidance, say so and do not present it as an official standard.
