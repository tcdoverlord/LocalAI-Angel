# Mathematics Foundations: K–12

## Number sense and arithmetic
Early learning focuses on counting, one-to-one correspondence, comparing quantities, place value, composing and decomposing numbers, and understanding the meanings of addition and subtraction. Multiplication can be introduced as equal groups, arrays, repeated addition, and area; division can be introduced as sharing equally and grouping. Fluency should grow alongside number sense rather than replace it.

## Fractions, decimals, and proportional thinking
A fraction describes a number of equal parts relative to a whole or a ratio. The denominator names the number of equal parts in the whole; the numerator names how many parts are considered. Equivalent fractions describe the same value. Decimal notation uses place value in powers of ten. Percent means “per hundred.” Ratios compare quantities, and unit rates describe an amount for one unit. Visuals such as number lines, area models, and double number lines can make these ideas concrete.

## Geometry and measurement
Foundational concepts include identifying and composing shapes, length, area, volume, angle, symmetry, coordinates, transformations, similarity, and congruence. Be explicit about units and whether a question asks for perimeter, area, or volume. A drawing may not be to scale unless the problem says so.

## Algebra and functions
Algebra represents patterns and relationships with symbols. A variable stands for a quantity; an expression represents a value; an equation states that two expressions are equal. Solving an equation means finding values that make the equality true, while maintaining equivalent operations on both sides. Functions describe an input-output relationship and can be represented with tables, graphs, rules, or verbal descriptions. Emphasize meaning and checking solutions, not just memorized steps.

## Statistics and probability
Students can learn to ask statistical questions, collect representative data, describe distributions, compare groups, understand variability, and interpret graphs without confusing correlation with causation. Probability measures uncertainty under a stated model or set of assumptions. Explain sample size, selection bias, outliers, and uncertainty when they affect a conclusion.

## A reliable problem-solving routine
1. Restate what is known and what must be found.
2. Identify useful representations: picture, table, number line, equation, or diagram.
3. Choose a method and explain why it fits.
4. Work step by step, showing units and intermediate reasoning.
5. Estimate the answer when practical.
6. Substitute, recompute, or use another method to check the result.

## Tutoring adaptation
For younger learners, use counters, drawings, and short number examples. For older learners, connect symbolic rules to graphs, models, and assumptions. Ask for the student's current grade or course and preferred method before assuming what approach is required. This guide is a general reference, not a claim about any particular district's standards.
