# Language Arts: Reading, Writing, Speaking, and Listening

## Reading comprehension
Effective reading combines decoding or word recognition, vocabulary, fluency, background knowledge, and comprehension. Readers identify the main idea, distinguish key details from examples, make reasonable inferences, recognize point of view, and cite textual evidence. For a literature question, ask which passage or edition the student is studying because wording and page numbers vary.

## Vocabulary and language
Teach unfamiliar words in context, explore roots and affixes, compare related words, and invite the learner to use a new word in an original sentence. Grammar and conventions matter because they clarify meaning, but editing should support the writer's purpose rather than erase their voice.

## Writing process
A flexible process is: understand the prompt, brainstorm, organize claims or ideas, draft, revise for meaning and evidence, edit for clarity and conventions, and proofread. Different genres use different structures. An argument should present a claim, relevant evidence, reasoning that connects evidence to the claim, and acknowledgment of important counterpoints when appropriate. Narrative writing develops a sequence of events, characters or perspective, detail, and reflection. Informative writing organizes accurate information for the reader.

## Source use and research
Students should identify who created a source, when it was published, what evidence it offers, what perspective it represents, and whether other credible sources corroborate it. Quotations should be exact and attributed; paraphrases must be genuinely restated and still cited when required. Do not fabricate page numbers, quotes, books, or source details. Follow the teacher's required citation style.

## Discussion and presentation
Good discussion includes listening, accurately representing another person's point, asking clarifying questions, and disagreeing with an idea respectfully. Presentations benefit from a clear purpose, a logical sequence, readable visuals, and practice timed for the assignment.

## Helping with an assignment
Ask for the exact directions and rubric when the task is course-specific. Offer an outline, feedback, questions, or a revision explanation that preserves the student's voice. If the purpose is assessment, prioritize coaching and hints rather than silently producing work the student is expected to complete independently.
